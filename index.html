The Attacking Wing-Back (AWB) Score

A composite score (0–100) measuring how suited a player is to an attacking wing-back role. The AWB score is built from 15 per-90 and percentage metrics across five categories, each internally weighted by how strongly its component metrics represent the category, then combined with category-level weights that prioritise creative output for defender-to-wing-back recruitment.

All raw per-90 values are scaled for league and team strength using Opta Power Rankings (α = 0.5) before z-scoring and normalisation. Percentage metrics are left unscaled since they already reflect rate-of-success against opposition.

Cosine Similarity to Mayor

Measures how closely a candidate's playing shape matches Mayor's, independent of absolute level. Calculated from 21 standardised (unscaled) metrics — the 15 AWB metrics plus 6 additional style-descriptive metrics that don't drive the AWB score but matter for role fit.
15 AWB metrics (as above) Crosses · Accurate crosses % · Dribbles · Successful dribbles % · Progressive runs · Key passes · xA · Shot assists · Accurate passes to penalty area / 90 · Defensive duels · Defensive duels won % · PAdj Interceptions · Successful defensive actions · Aerial duels · Aerial duels won % 6 additional style features Progressive passes / 90 · Received passes / 90 · Touches in box / 90 · Fouls suffered / 90 · Offensive duels won % · xG / 90
Each candidate receives a score between −1 and +1, where +1 would be an identical profile shape to Mayor. The six extra features (Passes / 90 and Offensive duels / 90 were dropped during analysis for correlation > 0.9 with existing features) capture build-up involvement, advanced-zone presence, and shooting tendency — axes the AWB composite doesn't measure.

Archetype Clustering

K-means (k = 4) on the same 21-feature space produces four playing-style groupings. Each player's "primary archetype" is the cluster centroid they are closest to in standardised multi-dimensional space. Fit percentages shown in the detail panel use a global distance scale: 100% would be the tightest observed fit in the sample, 0% the loosest.
